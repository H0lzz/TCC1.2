\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}

Preparing for public service and academic tests demands mastery of effective learning strategies and attention to students' mental well-being. However, currently available software solutions are fragmented, making it difficult for students to access a single application that supports them throughout this process. This work presents the theoretical and methodological grounding for the development of a free and open-source mobile application that integrates a Pomodoro timer, spaced-repetition flashcards, gamification, and mood check-ins. The methodology adopted primarily combines the Lean Inception framework for product vision alignment and the definition of a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) scope. Accordingly, requirements were gathered through proto-personas, user journeys, and feature brainstorming, alongside the definition of the system architecture, data model, and privacy and security measures aligned with Brazil's General Data Protection Law (LGPD). The literature review grounds the proposed features in academic evidence regarding time management, spaced repetition, gamification, and self-regulated learning. As a result, this work delivers a theoretically grounded, user-centered proposal encompassing a high-fidelity prototype, data modeling, architecture diagrams, and a roadmap for the development phase.

   \vspace{\onelineskip}

   \noindent
   \textbf{Key-words}: mobile application; time management; spaced repetition; gamification; self-regulated learning.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
